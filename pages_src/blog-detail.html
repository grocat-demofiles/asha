The History of Mysore Pak: Karnataka's Beloved Sweet

Mysore Pak is a rich, ghee-based sweet made from gram flour (besan), sugar, and ghee, known for its distinctive porous texture and melt-in-the-mouth quality. Originating in the royal kitchens of the Kingdom of Mysore in the early 20th century, it has become one of South India's most recognisable festive sweets — and a cornerstone of Asha Sweet Center's menu since 1951.

Origins in the Royal Kitchen

Mysore Pak is believed to have been created in the kitchens of the Mysore Palace during the reign of Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV, by a palace cook named Kakasura Madappa. Made originally for the royal family, the recipe used copious amounts of pure ghee — a sign of the dish's princely origins — and quickly spread beyond palace walls to sweet shops across Karnataka.

How It Got Its Name

The name combines "Mysuru" (the Kannada name for Mysore) and "paka," a Kannada/Telugu word referring to the sugar syrup consistency used in sweet-making. Together, the name simply means "sweet from Mysore" — a nod to its royal birthplace.

How Mysore Pak Is Made

At Asha Sweet Center, our Mysore Pak is prepared using a time-tested process passed down through three generations:

  • Gram flour is roasted lightly to remove raw taste while preserving its nutty aroma.
  • Sugar syrup is prepared to a precise one-string consistency.
  • Roasted gram flour is folded into the syrup along with hot, pure ghee, added in stages.
  • The mixture is continuously stirred until it reaches the right texture, then set and cut into pieces.

The ratio of ghee and the stirring technique determine whether the final texture is soft and porous or dense and chewy — both are popular in different parts of Karnataka.
